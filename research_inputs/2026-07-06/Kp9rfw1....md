In the latest episode of his podcast released on July 4, 2026, Anthony Pompliano hosts Jordy Visser for a Fourth of July-themed discussion focused on the current "midcycle slowdown" of the AI thematic trade and its implications for Bitcoin and the broader economy \[[00:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=45)\], \[[01:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=81)\].

### **The AI Midcycle Slowdown & Market Outlook**

Visser argues that the "easy money" phase of the AI trade is over \[[00:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=4)\]. He characterizes the current market as being in a "midcycle slowdown," where headwinds—such as increased government involvement, regulatory pressure, and concerns over compute availability—are becoming increasingly real \[[06:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=409)\], \[[07:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=459)\], \[[19:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=1154)\].

* **Volatility & Valuation:** Visser notes that tech momentum indices are currently experiencing volatility levels higher than any time in the last 25 years, including the dot-com bubble \[[53:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=3190)\]. This increased volatility makes it difficult for institutions to maintain long positions, leading to lower Sharpe ratios and a more challenging environment for equity investors \[[53:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=3215)\], \[[54:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=3266)\].  
* **The "Agentic" Shift:** While infrastructure and foundational models dominated the first phase of AI, Visser predicts that the next phase will be driven by the "agentic" side—AI agents that improve productivity and profit margins for enterprises \[[08:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=517)\], \[[26:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=1617)\].  
* **Memory as a Bottleneck:** Visser emphasizes that memory remains an insatiable, long-term bottleneck for AI growth. He views memory not as a bubble but as a fundamental need, projecting normalized returns of 30–40% per year rather than the extreme volatility seen previously \[[36:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=2212)\], \[[37:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=2256)\].

### **Bitcoin’s Bullish Case**

Despite a difficult year, Visser remains cautiously optimistic about Bitcoin, suggesting that its narrative is evolving \[[02:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=135)\], \[[05:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=347)\].

* **A Shift in Correlation:** Bitcoin has recently been lumped in with gold and silver as a "debasement" play, a narrative he finds increasingly interesting \[[03:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=190)\].  
* **Technical Triggers:** He highlights positive technical divergences and notes that Bitcoin held its lows despite significant selling in ETFs \[[03:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=203)\], \[[05:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=323)\].  
* **The Catalyst:** Visser suggests that if the labor market does not improve and the AI trade becomes more difficult for institutions to hold, Bitcoin's lower relative volatility may lead it into the next phase of its lifecycle—provided it can cross back above the 200-day moving average \[[05:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=347)\], \[[54:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=3271)\].

### **Macroeconomic & Regulatory Environment**

The discussion touches upon the changing role of the Federal Reserve and the potential for a "broadening out" of the market beyond the "Magnificent 7" \[[23:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=1423)\].

* **Fed Policy:** Visser suggests that the market has overreacted to hawkish sentiment. He posits that the Fed is likely to remain on hold and that inflation data may lag behind the actual productivity gains being driven by AI \[[45:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=2715)\], \[[48:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=2934)\].  
* **Geopolitics & Regulation:** He argues that American and Chinese competition will continue to drive innovation. While some fear that regulation or open-source competition will stifle American tech, Visser believes the focus should remain on how these technologies enable productivity and economic growth rather than just debating model sourcing \[[30:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=1837)\], \[[32:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp9rfw1Iqw8&t=1923)\].
