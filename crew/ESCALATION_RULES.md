# Escalation Rules

Escalation protects professional judgment and human authority.

## Escalation Levels

### Low

Low-risk issues are local, reversible, and low impact.

Expected handling:

- Role self-check
- Normal logging
- No human approval unless policy requires it

### Medium

Medium-risk issues affect quality, project direction, or recurring workflow behavior.

Expected handling:

- Specialist review
- Evidence summary
- CEO awareness
- Human approval if durable direction changes

### High

High-risk issues affect architecture, release readiness, external communication, destructive action, or disputed memory.

Expected handling:

- Multiple expert review
- CEO synthesis
- Human approval required
- Approval record required

### Critical

Critical issues are irreversible, legally sensitive, security-sensitive, financially material, reputation-impacting, or externally consequential.

Expected handling:

- Immediate CEO escalation
- Relevant specialist review
- Human approval required
- Alternatives and rollback path when applicable
- No silent fallback

## Escalation Triggers

- Evidence is missing for a consequential claim.
- Specialists materially disagree.
- Methodologies conflict.
- Human priorities are ambiguous.
- A decision is hard to reverse.
- A tool or provider failure affects evidence.
- A recommendation would bypass policy.
- Durable memory is disputed.

## Escalation Output

Escalations should include:

- Decision required
- Options
- Evidence
- Expert positions
- Risks
- Recommendation
- Confidence
- Consequences of delay
